# 69: Bug — the Record section's words vanish half-way through its dark-to-light blend

**What is wrong:** On the home page, scrolling into «السجل الموثّق», the section turns from dark to light by following the scroll: its ground and its words blend at the same rate, in opposite directions, and meet in the same grey. Stop scrolling there and the headline, the copy and the card's words cannot be read. It is the Reference site's own behaviour, carried over exactly — and it is not a Reference-site behaviour worth keeping. Once the section is light, the card's small grey lines and the unchosen record-type chips are faint as well.

**Blocked by:** nothing.

**Status:** ready-for-agent

**The founder sees it before it merges.** They chose this fix to try it, not yet to keep it. The pull request is not merged until they have scrolled through the section on its preview deployment, on a desktop and on a phone, and said yes.

- [ ] With motion on, scroll position alone can never leave the section in between dark and light: the headline, the copy, the chips and every word on the card read at 4.5:1 or more at every point the visitor can stop, on a desktop window and below 981px
- [ ] The section still holds the dark treatment as the visitor arrives, and switches to the light one on its own, over roughly half a second, once the visitor is 12.5% of the way in; scrolling back above that point switches it back the same way
- [ ] Section, words and card switch together, as one change
- [ ] With reduced motion nothing changes: one step, at the same point, as now
- [ ] On the light treatment, the card's second lines and times, the unchosen chips, and the stamp all read at 4.5:1 or more on their ground — with motion on and with it off
- [ ] The dark treatment looks exactly as it does now
- [ ] A test samples the section at points through its first third, once the switch has settled, and fails on today's blend

## Agent Brief

**Category:** bug
**Summary:** The Record section's scroll-scrubbed dark-to-light blend passes through a grey on which no text can be read; replace it with a switch at a scroll point that runs briefly on its own, and lift the light treatment's faint greys to AA.

**Current behaviour (confirmed 22 September 2026):**

With motion on, `RecordBehaviour` scrubs two GSAP tweens against the scroll, both `ease: 'none'`, both the Reference site's numbers verbatim:

- the section, from `--dark` ground and `--dark-text` words to paper `#FAFAF8` and ink `#222222`, over the first 25% of the section;
- the card, from `--dark2` to white, over 12%–32%. The card's words inherit the section's colour, so they blend on the section's schedule, not the card's.

Because words and ground cross the same grey at the same moment, contrast collapses. Measured by the tweens' own interpolation, and reproduced on the Reference page at 1440×900 (at 12% in, the headline rendered `rgb(140,140,143)` on `rgb(130,131,134)`):

| Where | Worst | At |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headline, four questions (on the section) | 1.12:1 | ~12% |
| Copy (`.lead`, at `.75` opacity) | 1.09:1 | ~12% |
| Card's step names | 1.10:1 | ~18% |
| Card's second lines and times (fixed `--dark-muted`) | 1.06:1 | ~24% |

Two more fail **at rest**, once the section is light, and with reduced motion too:

- The card's second lines and times keep `--dark-muted` (`#9AA0B4`) on white: **2.61:1**. Ticket 09 noted this and left it for the accessibility pass. That pass (ticket 36) did not see it, because axe reads the page as it arrives, and it arrives dark.
- The unchosen chips are `currentColor` at `.5` opacity: 4.71:1 on dark, **3.14:1** on light.

Reduced motion already avoids the grey: `.lit` is toggled in one step at 12.5% (ticket 09's divergence). That path is correct, apart from the two at-rest failures above.

**Why not just flip the words' colour:** while the ground still blends, some shades of it are at least 4.5:1 against neither the light words nor the dark ones. The best either can do on the worst of them is ~3.7:1. The ground itself must not be left in between.

**Desired behaviour:**

- One light treatment, one definition. The existing `lit` state (section on paper with ink words, card white with the `--line` border and `--acc-deep` ink for the stamp) becomes the light treatment for everyone. With motion on, the change into and out of it is a short CSS transition (about 0.4–0.5s) on the ground, the words, the card's ground and its border. With reduced motion it has no transition, as now.
- One trigger for both: 12.5% into the section, the point reduced motion already uses. Below 981px the percentages measure the shorter section, as they already do.
- The light treatment's text colours reach AA on their own ground: the card's second lines and times on white, and the unchosen chips on paper. Use the site's existing light-ground muted grey (`--muted`, 5.41:1 on white) instead of inventing a new one. The chips need whatever dimming reaches 4.5:1 on paper without changing how they look on dark.
- The five record types, the stamp's timing, and the header's colour toggle are unchanged.

**Tests to expect to change:** the Reference comparisons that sample colours part-way through the blend compare against the Reference site's grey on purpose. Those samples now differ by design. Record it as a deliberate divergence, as the section's other divergences are recorded, both in the behaviour's header comment and in the tests, rather than deleting the comparisons. The at-rest comparisons of the dark treatment should not change.

**Verify by falsification:** put the scrubbed tweens back and the new contrast test must fail. Put the dark-card greys back on the light treatment and the at-rest check must fail.

**Out of scope:**

- `reference/`: never edited. The same blend is in the Reference's `product.html` and `start.html`, but the rebuild has the section only on the home page.
- The card's divider rules on white. They are decorative, and WCAG's 3:1 for non-text applies to controls and meaningful graphics, not dividers.
- The brand-orange-on-accent exception (ADR-0011).
- Any other section's colours or motion.

**No ADR.** `reference/HANDOFF.md` only names this section, so this overrules nothing there. As with ticket 09's divergences, the reasoning lives in the code comment and here.
